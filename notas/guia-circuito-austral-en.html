Austral Circuit
12 days from the glaciers to the end of the world, across Argentina and Chile
← Home
🇦🇷 ES 🇬🇧 EN 🇧🇷 PT 🇨🇳 中文

One trip, two countries and five stops: the Perito Moreno Glacier in El Calafate, the granite towers of Torres del Paine, Puerto Natales, Punta Arenas on the Strait of Magellan, and Ushuaia, the southernmost city in the world. It is a 12-day overland route with two border crossings, heading south until it reaches the end of the continent. You can do it by bus or by rental car: pick up in El Calafate and drop off in Ushuaia, or the other way around.

When to go?

Nov · Dec
Start of the season

Long days, fewer people and lower prices. Some services are just opening and the wind is already strong in the park.

Jan · Feb
High summer

The best weather and all services open, but it is high season: book flights, buses and lodging well in advance.

Mar · Apr
Autumn: colors and calm

Reddish forests and fewer visitors. There are fewer bus departures and the penguin colony is no longer active.

Outside the summer season many services reduce their frequencies or do not run, especially the bus between Punta Arenas and Ushuaia and the boats. In winter, the W circuit and the Base of the Towers also require a CONAF-certified guide.

The five stops

Days 1 to 3 · El Calafate

It is the gateway to Los Glaciares National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Perito Moreno can be seen from the walkways, by boat or by walking on the ice. There is still time and energy left for Nimez lagoon and the town. See the El Calafate guide →

Days 5 to 7 · Torres del Paine

Three days are enough for the essentials: one day for the viewpoints over Lake Pehoé and the guanacos, another for the hike to the Base of the Towers (8 to 10 hours) and another for Grey Glacier. See the Torres del Paine guide →

Days 4 and 8 · Puerto Natales

It is the base for the park, about 110 km away: hostels, restaurants and gear shops. It serves as a stopover on the way in and out. It sits on Última Esperanza Sound, with views of the channels and the mountains in the background.

Days 8 and 9 · Punta Arenas

The largest city on the Strait of Magellan, with a waterfront of colorful houses and a stately main square. Excursions to the penguin colonies leave from here, and they run only in season: check dates and book before you travel.

Days 11 and 12 · Ushuaia

The southernmost city in the world, between the mountain range and the Beagle Channel. Two days are enough for Tierra del Fuego National Park and a cruise on the channel, with sea lions and lighthouses. See the Ushuaia guide →

What to eat along the way

Patagonian lamb

The dish of the whole route, grilled or stewed. You can eat it in El Calafate, Puerto Natales and Ushuaia, and at the estancias it is served with the barbecue in plain sight. For dessert, look for sweets and ice cream made with calafate, the berry that gives the town its name.

King crab

The queen of the Beagle and the Strait of Magellan. You can order it in Punta Arenas, Puerto Natales and above all Ushuaia, natural or gratin. It is an expensive dish: check the price before ordering.

Fuegian lamb and Beagle fish

In Ushuaia lamb is roasted on a cross, and there is also trout, Patagonian toothfish and shellfish from the cold waters of the channel. A dinner like this is the natural end to this trip.

Craft beer

El Calafate · Puerto Natales · Ushuaia

Every town has its brewery. It is a good stop after a long hike.

Pisco sour and Chilean wines

Puerto Natales · Punta Arenas

On the Chilean side, a pisco sour goes with almost any table. Wines from the Central Valley are on every menu.

Other attractions at each stop

El Calafate: beyond the glacier

Walk through the Laguna Nimez reserve, a few minutes from downtown, to see flamingos and lagoon birds. The Glaciarium, an ice museum on the road to the glacier, and the estancias where you can watch sheep shearing and taste a full barbecue make up a day without the glacier.

Optional excursion: El Chaltén

If you have an extra day, El Chaltén is about three hours from El Calafate and is Argentina's trekking capital, at the foot of Fitz Roy. It works as a day trip or with an overnight stay.

Puerto Natales: the Milodón Cave

About 25 km north of Puerto Natales, the remains of a giant ground sloth thousands of years old were found in the Milodón Cave. It is a short, half-day visit, a good addition to a rest day between hikes.

Punta Arenas: Fuerte Bulnes

About 55 km south of the city, on the strait, stands Fuerte Bulnes, founded in 1843: the first Chilean settlement in the area. What you visit today is a reconstruction, with wooden houses, a chapel and a palisade, and a good half-day outing along the coast.

Punta Arenas: penguins and the city

In season, the penguin colonies at Seno Otway or Isla Magdalena, reached by boat, are the most sought-after excursion. In the city, two hours are enough for Plaza Muñoz Gamero, the Municipal Cemetery and the Cerro de la Cruz viewpoint. Departures depend on the weather and the calendar: book in advance.

Ushuaia: the End of the World Train

A steam locomotive runs along a stretch of the old prison route, through lenga forests and along the Pipo River, inside Tierra del Fuego National Park. It is a ride of about two hours, very popular: book in advance in high season.

Ushuaia: channel and museums

The cruise on the Beagle reaches Les Éclaireurs Lighthouse and the islands of sea lions and cormorants. On land are the End of the World Museum and the former prison, now a maritime and prison museum. With time, Martial Glacier can be reached on a short walk from the city.

Laguna Esmeralda

A half-day hike through peat bogs and lenga forest to a turquoise lagoon at the foot of the glacier. It is the most recommended excursion for those who arrive eager to walk but without much energy.

Boat trips, penguin colony excursions and tourist trains run in season and depend on the weather. Book in advance and confirm schedules and prices with each operator.

How to get between stops: by bus

🚌

El Calafate – Puerto Natales

Bus with a border crossing: between 4 and 7 hours depending on the company and the delay at the checkpoint. In high season there are several departures a day; outside the season, fewer and not every day.

🚌

Puerto Natales – Punta Arenas

About 250 km, around 3 hours. There are many departures a day in summer and fewer in winter. You buy tickets at the terminal or online.

⛴️

Punta Arenas – Ushuaia

The longest leg: 10 to 12 hours by bus, with a ferry crossing of the Strait of Magellan (about 20 minutes) and a border check at San Sebastián. The companies do not run every day and the ferry depends on the weather.

✈️

Flying as an alternative

If time is short, you can fly between Punta Arenas and Ushuaia or from El Calafate to the rest of the country, and keep the bus only for the shorter legs. Flights change by season: check before planning the trip.

Crossing the Strait of Magellan

To reach Tierra del Fuego you have to cross the Strait on a ferry, at the Primera Angostura crossing, between Punta Delgada and Bahía Azul. It carries buses, cars and trucks, and the crossing takes about 20 minutes. If it is very windy, it is delayed or cancelled: that is why it is wise to leave some margin in the day.

Border: this route crosses between Argentina and Chile twice: when going from El Calafate to Puerto Natales and when going from Punta Arenas to Ushuaia. Bring your passport (or the valid document for your nationality) and do not carry fresh fruit, meat, dairy or seeds into Chile: they are checked and confiscated. Always check requirements, schedules and the status of the crossings on official sites before you leave.

Doing it by rental car

The freest option: pick up the vehicle in El Calafate and drop it off in Ushuaia, or the other way around. It is possible, but with conditions. Both cities are in Argentina, and rental companies require the car to return to Argentina; the hard part is crossing into Chile and getting authorization to do so.

📄

Permit to cross into Chile

It is mandatory and must be requested from the company in advance: Hertz requires at least 72 hours, with a copy of your passport and driver's license. You need a notarized authorization, insurance valid in both countries and the vehicle's papers. Without a permit, insurance can be voided.

🚗

Not all companies or all cars

According to travel guides, Hertz, Localiza and Europcar authorize the crossing for a fee; Budget decides case by case and most local companies do not allow it. At Hertz, small economy models cannot cross and the authorized fleet in Ushuaia is limited.

🏁

Dropping off in another city

Between El Calafate and Ushuaia there is a one-way fee that does not appear in published rates: ask for the total price in writing before booking. Returning the car outside Argentina carries penalties of thousands of dollars at Hertz.

⛽

What adds up

Fuel, tolls and the Strait ferry (paid separately), and full insurance, advisable on gravel: a windshield can cost between 300 and 800 dollars.

In summer, a compact SUV is enough, crosses into Chile with a permit and uses less fuel. The roads inside Torres del Paine are gravel and have strong crosswinds.

Practical tips

See a doctor before you travel and train in advance if you plan to hike in Torres del Paine. See the notice at the end of this guide: Physical demands and preparation.
Book first what sells out: lodging in Torres del Paine, park tickets (pasesparques.cl) and the boats to the glacier.
Leave a spare day before any return flight: long bus rides and the ferry can be delayed by wind.
Carry some cash in Argentine and Chilean pesos, especially in the park and in small towns.
Pack in layers: in a single day you can go from sun to icy wind. A light waterproof jacket is a must.
Confirm schedules, border crossings and opening dates with official sources and with the bus companies: they change every season.

Physical demands and preparation

⚠️ Before you book, make sure you are fit for it

This route combines many hours of overland travel with hikes in Torres del Paine: the Base of the Towers takes between 8 and 10 hours, with a climb over rock, strong wind and fast-changing weather. Medical services are far from several stretches.

  • Get a full medical check-up before traveling and ask whether your health is suitable for long hikes and so many hours of travel. This is especially important if you have heart or blood pressure problems, respiratory conditions, knee or back problems, diabetes, or take medication.
  • Train in advance: long walks with a backpack, plus stairs or hills, for several weeks before the trip.
  • Consider going with a certified guide for the hikes, especially if you lack experience or travel in winter.
  • Buy travel insurance with medical and rescue coverage that includes trekking.
  • Listen to your body: if you do not feel well, rest or turn back. The trip will not run out.

This guide is informational and does not replace the opinion of a doctor or a professional guide. Everyone must assess their own condition and that of those traveling with them.

Torres del Paine
The granite heart of Chilean Patagonia
← Home
🇨🇱 ES 🇬🇧 EN 🇧🇷 PT 🇨🇳 中文

Torres del Paine is the most famous park in Chilean Patagonia and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 1978. Across some 240,000 hectares in the Magallanes region, about 110 km from Puerto Natales, you find granite towers, glaciers, turquoise lakes and wildlife that lets itself be seen: guanacos, condors and free-roaming pumas. You can explore it on a one-day hike or on multi-day circuits.

When to go?

Oct · Nov
Spring: fewer crowds

Thaw, wildflowers and quieter trails. The wind is already strong, and some services and the O Circuit are only just starting their season: confirm dates before booking.

Dec · Feb
Summer: high season

Very long days and the best weather, but also peak visitor numbers. Refuges and campsites sell out months in advance.

Mar · Apr
Autumn: colors and calm

The forest turns red and yellow, with fewer people and cold nights. A favorite time for photographers.

In winter a special regulation applies: the W Circuit and the trail to the Base of the Towers require a CONAF-certified guide.

How to get there

✈️

By air

Most visitors fly to Punta Arenas or El Calafate (Argentina) and continue overland to Puerto Natales, the most convenient base for visiting the park.

🚌

From Puerto Natales

About 110 km to the Laguna Amarga entrance: two hours by bus or car, with daily services in high season.

🛂

From El Calafate

Bus with a border crossing: between 4 and 7 hours to Puerto Natales, with fewer departures in winter. Bring your passport or valid travel document.

⛴️

Pudeto – Paine Grande catamaran

It crosses Lake Pehoé and is the western gateway to the W Circuit. It depends on weather and wind: confirm schedules on the day.

Tickets: since May 1, 2026 a new system applies (a daily ticket, or a ticket for 2 days or more, valid for up to 10 consecutive days). Buying in advance is mandatory at pasesparques.cl and you show the QR code at the entrance. Prices for foreign visitors change: check the official website.

What to do

Base of the Towers

The park's postcard. You reach it from Hotel Las Torres on a trail of about 20 km round trip, which takes 8 to 10 hours. The last stretch, a climb over moraine, is the toughest. At the top, the three granite towers rise above a turquoise lagoon. To see the reddish sunrise you need to sleep at the campsite closest to the base and climb before dawn.

The W Circuit

The most famous trek, shaped like a W: Grey Glacier, French Valley with the British viewpoint, and the Base of the Towers. It takes 4 or 5 days, with refuges or campsites along the way. Many start at Paine Grande, reached by catamaran from Pudeto, and finish at the Base of the Towers; it can also be done in reverse.

The O Circuit and John Gardner Pass

The full circuit around the Paine massif: it adds the northern leg to the W and crosses John Gardner Pass (over 1,200 m), with views of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field. It takes 7 to 9 days on much quieter trails. Like the W, it requires booking accommodation in advance.

Grey Glacier

A blue ice front more than 6 km wide on Lake Grey, part of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field. You can see it from the trail viewpoints (about 3 to 4 hours from Paine Grande) or by boat up to the glacier front. It is the western end of the W Circuit.

Lake Pehoé and the Cuernos del Paine

For those not hiking for several days: from the main road you reach the viewpoints over Lake Pehoé and Salto Grande, with the Cuernos del Paine in the background. It is the park's classic photo and can be done on a day trip by car from Puerto Natales.

Los Cuernos and Lake Nordenskjöld

Between the French Valley and the Towers, the W Circuit skirts Lake Nordenskjöld, with the Cuernos del Paine as a backdrop. The Los Cuernos and Francés campsites are along this stretch.

Wildlife: guanacos, condors and pumas

The park is one of the best places in the world to see Patagonian wildlife with no fences: herds of guanacos on the steppe, condors above the cliffs, rheas and, with luck, pumas, especially around Lake Sarmiento and Laguna Amarga. Always keep your distance.

Where to sleep

Refuges and campsites on the W and O

Inside the park

They are run by private concessionaires and CONAF. To hike the W or the O, booking accommodation in advance is mandatory: without it you cannot enter the mountain area.

Paine Grande Refuge

West end of Lake Pehoé

The starting point of the W and the arrival point of the catamaran. Beds, meals and camping.

Hotel Las Torres

Access to the Base of the Towers

The hotel closest to the classic trail. It reopened for the season on October 1, 2026.

Hostería Pehoé and Hotel Lago Grey

Lake Pehoé · Lake Grey

Options with beds for those who prefer sleeping indoors without doing the circuits.

Puerto Natales

About 110 km from the park

The most practical base: hostels, hotels, restaurants and gear shops. Daily buses to the park leave from here, and day trips are organized.

Practical tips

See a doctor before you travel and train in advance: the W and O treks are demanding. See the notice at the end of this guide: Physical demands and preparation.
Buy your ticket in advance at pasesparques.cl and save the QR code: it is checked at the entrance.
Book refuges and campsites as soon as you set your dates. It is mandatory for the W and O, and in summer they sell out months ahead.
Wind can exceed 100 km/h and the weather changes within hours. Bring layers, a waterproof jacket and a hat even in summer.
Fires are forbidden outside designated areas. Fires have devastated the park in the past.
Carry cash in Chilean pesos: card payments inside the park do not always work.
Always check official sources before you set out: parquetorresdelpaine.cl, conaf.cl and pasesparques.cl. Prices, schedules and opening dates change every season.

Physical demands and preparation

⚠️ Before you book, make sure you are fit for it

The Torres del Paine hikes are demanding: many hours a day carrying a backpack, steep sections, rocky and muddy ground, strong wind and fast-changing weather. Medical services are far away, and a health problem in the middle of a circuit can become serious.

  • Get a full medical check-up before traveling and ask whether your health is suitable for long, demanding hikes. This is especially important if you have heart or blood pressure problems, respiratory conditions, knee or back problems, diabetes, or take medication.
  • Train in advance: long walks with a loaded backpack, plus stairs or hills, for several weeks before the trip.
  • Consider going with a certified guide, especially if it is your first multi-day hike or you go in winter, when a guide is mandatory on the W and the Base of the Towers.
  • Buy travel insurance with medical and rescue coverage. Check that it includes trekking activities.
  • Tell someone your route and respect the quota and dates of your reservations. Some areas have little or no mobile signal.
  • Listen to your body: if you do not feel well, turn back. The mountain will still be there.

This guide is informational and does not replace the opinion of a doctor or a professional guide. Everyone must assess their own condition and that of those traveling with them.
